Set up a crash, see the damage

Choose the speed, where the car is hit, what it hits and what the car is made of. See which parts are crushed or torn off and which effects a game should show. Everything runs in your browser.

Please read first. No real crash data was used. The AI model learned from 8,000 crashes simulated by a small physics layer, and that layer was never compared with crash tests. The result sketches how a crash could look. It is not a safety rating.

      How it works

      The physics layer

      A simple energy model. The crash energy follows from the speed and the masses. The contact patch decides which of the car's 28 boxes are in the line of the hit, and each part crushes at a force set by its material, thickness and size. The weaker body, car or obstacle, gives way first. Damage then spreads to connected parts, such as door to pillar to windscreen.

      The AI model

      A graph neural network. It reads the same inputs and predicts the damage of every part in one pass. It was trained on 8,000 random crashes labelled by the physics layer.

      What it is not

      Neither engine was compared with real crash tests, the constants are chosen for plausible sizes rather than fitted, and the car is a single compact layout. A narrow obstacle such as a post is the weakest case: every part it touches counts as heavily damaged, even when only a strip of that part is hit. Use it to explore ideas and to prepare effects for games, not to judge safety.
